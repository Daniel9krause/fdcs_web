The problem

Smallholder farmers lose a large share of their harvest to pests and diseases every season, and some conditions, such as aflatoxin mould, make food dangerous to eat. Extension officers are few and far between, and many problems look alike to the untrained eye.

Our solution

A phone camera and a trained CNN. {{ cfg.APP_NAME }} recognises {{ model_info.classes - 1 }} pests and diseases plus healthy crops, classifies the organism responsible, gives a safe-to-eat verdict and management advice, and stores every photo in a crop gallery that can be exported to retrain the model.

Limitations

The model recognises only the conditions it was trained on and works best on clear, close-up photos; unfamiliar crops are reported as "not recognised". The safe-to-eat verdict is based on what is visible: it cannot detect pesticide residues or invisible toxins. The app supports farmers' decisions but does not replace an extension officer or laboratory testing.
